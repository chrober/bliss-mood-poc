## Design intent

The goal is **one adaptive strategy which discovers effective concurrency on its actual host and workload**, not a Ryzen-specific fixed-worker recommendation. The existing 100-song/3-model native C++ benchmark gave median 6=70.98, 8=70.42, 10=63.75 and 12=88.17 songs/minute (two repeats). That is a **nonmonotone observed throughput curve**, with possible environmental noise. A hill-climbing rule that abandons higher counts after a worse point is unsuitable. No claim of causality, universal optimum, or safe fixed-twelve default follows.

Separate mechanisms:

1. **Admission safety / resource governor:** use the CPU capacity permitted to the process as a hard upper bound (maximum 64) and measure available memory before starting. Compute a conservative initial reserve, here `max(512 MiB, 10% of initially available memory)`, and charge **at least 256 MiB estimated incremental memory per added worker**. Reduce the preferred initial concurrency if its estimated budget would be exceeded, rather than assuming `--workers auto` is always safe. Require valid free-RAM telemetry on every completion, **and run an independent 250-ms memory watchdog while a song is in flight**. A missing/invalid sample or a breached reserve is a terminal safety fault: the watchdog signals native IPC cancellation, the coordinator stops dispatch and checks its guard during 200-ms result waits, and the owning Rust threads kill/reap their native Essentia child processes (rather than killing bare PIDs). A completion-driven failure also signals cancellation immediately. On lesser headroom pressure, the online tuner reduces concurrency instead of launching additional sessions. Raise the incremental-memory estimate whenever observed host/cgroup memory depletion implies higher costs; never relax the estimate within that scan. These **provisional guardrails** are portable heuristics to test, **not a guarantee against OOM**. The watchdog is independent of song completions but still samples periodically, so rapid allocations can exceed its margin between polls. Native C++ IPC can be cancelled promptly using the owning Child handle; already-running Rust ONNX computation is **not** preemptible yet. The current design cannot guarantee that an operating system or cgroup OOM killer will not intervene first. Failures may leave a *partial, private* sidecar rather than silently continuing.

2. **Online throughput exploration:** an ordered **coarse candidate schedule** includes minimum, initial, maximum and evenly spaced intermediate worker counts, with no assumption of monotonicity. Each candidate needs at least eight successfully completed songs and twelve seconds of data before a probe is accepted. Score is analyzed EffNet patches/second, alongside songs/minute, rather than raw CPU load (which is not a throughput objective). The initial coarse sweep proceeds through the safe candidates even across a throughput trough; the **second sweep runs in reverse order**, including a genuine second probe for a candidate which is already active at the sweep boundary. This reduces systematic time drift and supplies a second observation per feasible candidate before ordinary exploitation. Unsafe candidates may be memory-gated, so two samples are **not guaranteed**. A **bounded rolling median** of up to eight recent probes per worker count replaces single-best-window selection. After the first sweep, a solitary surprising new score cannot displace a previously observed incumbent without replication. Explore higher candidates even after a lower candidate performs worse. Use a 5% improvement margin when selecting the best observed candidate, to limit insignificant switches. For longer scans, return to the best observed candidate between budgeted re-exploration rounds. Each candidate is separately RAM-gated. The controller adjusts only the **in-flight dispatch cap**; native/ONNX sessions are persistent and never restarted for re-sizing. **Every change now drains the prior configuration's in-flight jobs before admitting new work.** The measurement epoch starts *after* this drain, so completions dispatched under an earlier capacity cannot contaminate the new capacity's probe. This improves attribution, but model warmup, changing songs, temporary idling and the overhead of draining still affect total end-to-end completion time.

3. **Diagnostics:** the existing `diagnostics.adjustments` and `diagnostics.controller_decisions` belong to the legacy policy. Experimental observations live under `diagnostics.online_autotune`: actual starting/final workers, retained coarse probes and worker adjustments (maximum **256 each** for bounded full-library output), RAM reserve and worker estimate, candidate changes with available RAM, and probe/safety counters. Per-candidate rolling evidence includes sample count, median and observed min/max patch rate. `search_completed` means every candidate was sampled at least once; `replication_completed` additionally requires two retained samples each; `observed_ranges_separated` is a **descriptive** test requiring the winner's observed minimum to exceed every competitor's observed maximum by over 5%. These are **not formal statistical confidence intervals or guarantees of the true optimum**; `best_tested_workers` is the current highest-ranked sampled candidate.

## Invocation (experimental)

```text
bliss-mood scan-features ... --workers auto --adaptive-workers \
  --adaptive-policy autotune --min-workers 1 --max-workers auto \
  --threads 1 --priority normal --json
```

Windows `--priority normal` is only for explicitly controlled comparisons; omit it for portable Linux usage. Use an explicit bounded `--max-new`, a fresh sidecar, and matching model/native worker versions for research. The legacy mode remains `--adaptive-policy resource` (default), with the existing minimum-song gate and 20-second cooldown.

Linux free RAM now combines `/proc/meminfo` with **visible nested cgroup ancestry**: it parses the process's unified v2 or legacy memory-v1 membership from `/proc/self/cgroup`, walks accessible ancestors under the standard `/sys/fs/cgroup` mounts, and uses the **smallest visible remaining memory headroom**, not merely the host-wide figure. A unit test checks the namespace-root path and rejects traversal. This is **not yet a full cgroup namespace/mountinfo resolution**: custom mount roots, unavailable ancestors, swap quotas, CPU throttling and macOS remain unhandled. Where free RAM cannot be read, `autotune` refuses to begin. Tests must cover cgroup variation, failure handling and 4-GB Raspberry Pi constraints before enabling it by default.

## New opt-in FFmpeg subprocess protection (28 October 2026)

The **Rust audio frontend only**, when invoked under `autotune` with a scan cancellation token, now supervises the child processes used for both `ffprobe` and `ffmpeg`. This is separate from the original native Essentia C++ frontend's already-supervised IPC child.

- Own a direct subprocess handle, poll it at 100-ms intervals, kill/reap it on scan cancellation or bounded-output fault, and ensure early error exits also reclaim child ownership. Pipe readers run separately for stdout/stderr to avoid filled-pipe deadlocks. There is no claim that this kills grandchildren or arbitrary external helpers.
- Limit `ffprobe` stdout/stderr to 1 MiB each; bound `ffmpeg` stderr to 1 MiB. Cap decoded Float32 PCM bytes using current **effective host/cgroup free RAM divided by four times the configured maximum scan worker pool**, further capped at 512 MiB per worker. This is a conservative **heuristic**, not proof of an OOM-safe reservation: allocator capacity, simultaneous ONNX sessions, other working arrays and sudden external load still matter. Songs exceeding the budget fail explicitly, not silently truncate.
- The **historical non-autotune decoder still uses its original blocking `.output()` calls and mathematically identical libsamplerate-compatible resampling**, avoiding behavioral changes to the validated resource/fixed scan paths. A synthetic 16-kHz mono WAV test compares old and guarded decoder output bit-for-bit when FFmpeg is installed; separate subprocess tests cover cancellation, exact stdout/stderr capture, bounded output and, on Windows, a blocked PowerShell subprocess. This is not yet a full private real-music three-head/1280D parity result.
- Output counters are scan scoped; cancelling one scanner does not cancel another scanner. Existing model/source identities and read-only database access are unchanged.

The proposed **hard-kill ONNX feature-worker process architecture** is documented separately in [onnx-worker-process-isolation.md](onnx-worker-process-isolation.md). That document is a design and migration/testing plan; a killable ONNX subprocess implementation has **not** yet been added. Already running native ONNX Runtime `Session::run()` or `commit_from_file()` remains non-preemptible in the current Rust worker threads.

## Why this is only Phase B groundwork

- **A short scan cannot identify an optimum.** Coarse candidates can remain untested; no success claim is allowed. The extra initialization cost of rarely used ONNX sessions can hurt short-job throughput.
- A fixed-duration window over *different songs* and NAS caching does not give a statistically comparable experiment. The two balanced sweeps and bounded rolling medians reduce sensitivity to some timing artifacts, but do not establish exchangeable, randomized or independent samples. Patch count is a useful denominator, but decode cost, song complexity and ambient interference vary. **The new drain boundary prevents mixing old and new admission caps, not confounding from the song mix.** Do not mistake a single 5% margin for statistical significance. Moreover, measuring probes after drain excludes switching overhead from the scored patch rate; validate actual full-scan wall time as an independent goal.
- **Implemented:** independent watchdog at ~250 ms, coordinator polling at ~200 ms on outstanding completions, explicit native IPC cancellation and child reaping on a memory fault, interruptible bounded FFmpeg/ffprobe subprocesses for autotune's Rust decoder, and additional `diagnostics.memory_watchdog` with sampled low-water RAM. **Cancellation is now scoped to a single scan** (shared `Arc<AtomicBool>` propagated from the watchdog to the scan's worker threads, native Essentia IPC, and ONNX analysis engine); no process-global flag can inadvertently cancel a different scan. The native transport checks it during each ≤200-ms blocking wait, and the Rust frontend checks at entry, after decoding/preprocessing, before/after encoder and classification calls, between patches, and around hashing/persistence handoff. **Model initialization now receives the same cancellation token at construction time** and checks before/after process environment setup and each encoder/head session load. These checks avoid starting the next session after a fault, but cannot interrupt a native ONNX Runtime session load already in progress. A deterministic test exercises independent watchdogs, and a fixture-backed test triggers cancellation while a real mock C++ worker is blocked in IPC, verifies reaping, and checks that an unrelated worker still processes a request. **Still missing:** preemption of a single already running ONNX Runtime call **or ONNX model-session load**, hard-kill confinement of worker/grandchild process trees, measured per-session allocation accounting, full cgroup OOM event inspection, sub-poll allocation protection, and a platform-tested hard stop for in-flight nonnative computation. The new stage checks are cooperative and must not be described as guaranteed immediate interruption or OOM protection.
- The candidate spacing is provisional and can miss narrow peaks. Explore/refine adaptively with paired/interleaved comparisons, repeated probes, recency-aware confidence estimates and change-detection, while charging search cost. Resource isolation must always dominate optimization.
- Benchmark *completed-song throughput* and *time-to-finish* along with patches/sec on long, heterogeneous bounded cohorts and on differing host budgets. Synthetic tests must include 6→8→10→12 with a local trough, increasing monotone, decreasing, flat/noisy, changing workload, and low-RAM situations. Verify strict classification / embedding parity with the original native C++ frontend.
- Test on CPU-constrained containers and ARM/4-GB machines before generalizing. Defaults must remain stable and fail safe; PR #16 similarity work and original library/Bliss DB stay untouched.

## Algorithm-first development in PR #19 (28 October 2026)

The project's priority is **safe, hardware-independent, nonmonotonic online throughput optimization**, not further ONNX-process infrastructure. Only opt-in adaptive-policy autotune is changed. The legacy resource controller, original Essentia and ONNX math, SQLite single-writer and PR #16 are unchanged.

### Current integration and findings

The scan_parallel coordinator in src/features.rs instantiates OnlineAutotuner only when autotune is explicitly enabled. On each persisted completion, save_completion sends cumulative successfully analyzed songs, EffNet patch counts, effective available RAM and remaining scan size to observe(). A changed worker capacity stops new admission until prior in-flight work drains; settle() resets measurement attribution. The RAM watchdog and CPU/cgroup upper bounds remain safety guards.

The existing coarse candidate schedule **does not stop at local declines**. For 6..12 workers it visits 6, 8, 10 and 12. Its existing forward/reverse sweeps, >=8-song / >=12-second measurement windows, rolling median of up to eight observations and 5% improvement hysteresis are preserved.

### Prioritized algorithm changes

| Priority | Weakness | Current response |
|---|---|---|
| P0 | Long exploration can consume a short scan | **Implemented:** after each real probe, budget further search against observed songs/window and remaining scan work, reserving roughly two windows worth of work per outstanding candidate; stop exploring when that is unaffordable. This is a bounded heuristic, not an ROI proof. |
| P0 | Previously fastest count can be unsafe after free RAM falls | **Implemented:** exploitation and scan-tail fallback recheck incremental-worker RAM admissibility and choose only a safe sampled count. Never bypass the watchdog. |
| P1 | Coarse stride can miss a narrow local maximum | **Implemented:** after two nonmonotone coarse sweeps, and only when budget allows, probe at most the two previously unsampled immediate neighbors of the replicated winner, twice each. No requirement of monotonicity or special hardware constants. |
| P1 | Song mix, NAS caches, warmup and changing background load distort comparisons | **Next:** paired/interleaved incumbent reference windows, per-window patch/song mix and load covariates, startup and drain accounting, explicit drift detection with bounded resampling. Existing reverse sweep/rolling median alone do not establish an unbiased comparison. |
| P1 | Patch/s is not end-to-end total scan speed | **Next:** benchmark matched complete scans against safe fixed-worker baselines, recording wall-clock seconds, completed songs/min, patches/s, startup/drain, RAM, CPU, IO and errors. |
| P2 | Narrow peaks beyond immediate neighbors or changing workload regimes | Consider multi-resolution/local stochastic probing after the bounded first refinement, driven by remaining-work payback and time-aware uncertainty; no endless sweeps. |

### Deterministic evidence and acceptance

New Rust simulations use the actual observe()/settle() production state transitions, with virtual time and no music or model data. The concrete **6:70.98, 8:70.42, 10:63.75, 12:88.17 songs/min** curve reaches 12 despite both intermediate declines and selects 12 with sufficient remaining workload and RAM. Separate tests cover monotone increasing/decreasing, flat/noisy curves, rolling evidence after sustained load drift, falling/recovering effective RAM, short scans that stop exploration, long scans that afford repeated sweeps, and a sharp optimum at 11 between coarse candidates 10 and 12. A 400-song synthetic whole-scan calculation includes an illustrative two-second switching penalty and is expressly **not a measured end-to-end speedup**.

**Verified:** source commit 205c592f, Rust Actions run 40695839304: **96 unit tests, 15 integration tests, zero failures**, including the narrow-11 peak; earlier commit d954d3b8 run 40695116657: 95 unit and 15 integration tests PASS. These establish deterministic *algorithm behavior only*. Other CI at the latest exact source SHA must be checked independently.

### Next experiments before performance claims

1. Add explicit observed drain, session-warmup, preprocessing and persistent-write costs to search accounting. Test mixed song lengths and patches/song; compare incumbent and challenger on interleaved work rather than trusting separated consecutive windows.
2. Test switching under synthetic background-load regime changes and RAM drops/recovery, with bounded candidate revisits. Report regret/uncertainty and stop conditions, not simply the highest instantaneous sample.
3. Run bounded, matched-order full-scan A/B studies for autotune versus safe fixed counts on Ryzen, Pi 4B and CPU-/RAM-limited hosts. Use identical model hashes, audio metadata, scan manifests and cache conditions; record all stages and complete elapsed time. No single hardware-specific optimum is assumed.
4. Preserve existing process-isolation progress but do not extend that architecture until directly required by a demonstrated autotuning safety or measurement problem. Default resource remains unchanged.


## Reproducible full-scan autotune-versus-fixed benchmark protocol (28 October 2026)

The preexisting **local-only** `scripts/benchmark_native_adaptive_manifest.py` now supports a dedicated `--experiment autotune-e2e`. This is **not** another synthetic 12-worker optimizer test: the harness launches the actual, unchanged `scan-features` executable against the **same bounded 30–500 real tracks** in each experimental run, with the original native Essentia-C++ worker, all selected official ONNX heads, **new independent SQLite feature sidecars** and embedding directories. The Bliss cohort DB and source music are read-only; input manifest, raw reports, embeddings and model material stay private on the host.

**Comparison design:** Explicit `--fixed-workers` baselines (at least two safe, distinct worker counts), plus the actual `--adaptive-workers --adaptive-policy autotune` with independent `--autotune-min`, `--autotune-initial`, `--autotune-max` bounds. No worker number is hardcoded as an optimum. The harness checks that the initial worker count is one of the fixed baselines and all counts lie in the same specified range and the host CPU budget. Existing fixed/resource-policy modes remain unchanged. Modes execute in a deterministic randomized order within the first round and in reverse order in the second round; repeat twice or four times. **Actual elapsed wall seconds** are measured externally with `time.perf_counter()` from before spawning the process through its exit, thus retaining pre-worker CPU baseline, ONNX startup, native subprocess startup, decoding, NAS cache, drain and SQLite persistence. Pre-worker baseline-excluded seconds are *diagnostic only*, not the main score.

**Correctness and safety:** The existing Windows host-RAM governor checks physical available RAM before and during every real run and terminates the native scanner process tree on pressure. The scanner's own `autotune` RAM gate and watchdog remain active. The harness fails on errors, missing tracks, priority mismatches, out-of-range workers or missing autotune diagnostics; every run's **exact 56/87/2 classifier-score blobs**, **complete pooled 1280D embedding data** and model identities/fingerprints must match across worker counts and modes. Every run gets a fresh sidecar; no scan result is reused. The run record reports wall seconds, completed songs/min, whole-scan patches/s, startup baseline, cumulative decode/preprocess/model load/inference, process-tree RAM, observed worker adjustments, tuner probe counts and search/replication flags.

**Evaluation:** The `summary.json` now includes per-run, round-paired speedup percentages against *every fixed baseline*, with a transparent sign convention: `100 × (fixed_wall / autotune_wall - 1)`, positive when autotune finishes faster. It also reports the median of these paired percentages and how many rounds autotune won. **Two/four repeats are descriptive; they are not confidence intervals or proof of an improvement** under NAS caching or background load. Compare *total full-scan elapsed time* first, and only then explain outcomes using patch rate and controller telemetry. A trial with no tuning probes or without a completed search is informative about short-scan overhead but does not establish optimal-search behavior.

## Erster abgeschlossener privater Native-End-to-End-Vergleich und Suchbudget-Korrektur (28.10.2026)

Der erste reale lokale `autotune-e2e`-Benchmark ist **abgeschlossen**, kein synthetisches Szenario: **10 komplette Scans**, identische **100 Songs** und **27.617 EffNet-Patches** je Lauf, originale Essentia-C++-Worker, drei ONNX-Heads und vollständige Embedding-Ausgabe, zwei umgekehrt ausbalancierte Wiederholungen pro Modus. Das Python-Harness bestätigte `exact_numeric_parity=true`; die RAM-Sicherheitsüberwachung brach keinen Lauf ab. Nur diese **aggregierten Ergebnisse** werden dokumentiert; keine privaten DBs, Songlisten, Rohreports oder Embeddings ins Repo.

| Betrieb | Median komplette Wall-Clock-Zeit | Median Songs/min | Max. gemessene private Prozessbaum-MiB |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| fixed6 | 90,079 s | 66,616 | 625,7 |
| fixed8 | 82,562 s | 72,997 | 807,2 |
| fixed10 | 73,055 s | 82,192 | 1.030,0 |
| fixed12 | **70,560 s** | **85,038** | 1.215,6 |
| autotune (min/start/max 6/6/12) | 87,559 s | 68,707 | 957,5 |

Mit dem alten opt-in-Code war Autotune in diesen beiden vollständigen 100-Song-Scans **19,231% langsamer als fixed12** nach dem gepaarten Speedup-Maß, **16,226% langsamer als fixed10**, **5,781% langsamer als fixed8** und etwa **3,208% schneller als fixed6**. Nur zwei Wiederholungen, keine statistische Signifikanz und keine universellen Hardware-Regeln. Unabhängig vom Modus waren mindestens 4.237 MiB freier physischer RAM beobachtet worden, weshalb kein RAM-Gate den Autotune-Suchabbruch verursacht hat.

**Gezielter Fehlernachweis mit lokalen, aggregierten Autotune-Messfenstern:** Beide Autotune-Läufe hatten **6 Probes**, **0 Workerwechsel**, `search_completed=false`, `completed_coarse_sweeps=0`, `exploration_budget_holds=1`, nur **eine getestete Workerzahl (6)**. Die **erste** Messung umfasste jeweils **12 Songs und ~12,5 s**. Der alte Controller evaluierte das verbleibende Arbeitsbudget **vor** dem Überspringen des bereits gemessenen Startkandidaten und rechnete **4 Kandidaten × 12 Songs × Faktor 2 = 96 noch nicht dispatchte Songs**. Nach zwölf abgeschlossenen Songs sind höchstens 88 Songs im ganzen Cohort übrig, und noch weniger sind undispatcht. Die Bedingung konnte daher in **beiden** Durchläufen nicht erfüllt werden; der Controller setzte einen permanenten Exploration-Stop, ohne jemals 8, 10 oder 12 zu probieren. Dies ist **ein generischer Budgetierungsfehler**, kein lokales Optimum bei 6 und keine spezielle Zwölf-Worker-Schwäche.

**Implementierungskorrektur auf PR #19:** Die erste, bislang unvollständige grobe Exploration budgetiert nun nur **noch nie gemessene** Kandidaten, je ein **beobachtet großes** Messfenster, plus **ein Messfenster für tatsächliche spätere Ausnutzung**. Bei den gemessenen 12 Songs je erster Probe und drei verbleibenden Kandidaten ist das **4 × 12 = 48 undispatchte Songs statt 96**. Ein zweiter Sweep zur Replikation oder lokale Feinabstimmung bleiben optional und werden separat gegen das verbleibende Arbeitsbudget geprüft. Dynamische RAM-Sicherheitsprüfung und unverändertes `resource`-Verhalten bleiben harte Grenzen. Diese **allgemeine Regel** ist weder nach Workerzahl noch nach Plattform abgestimmt.

Ein neuer deterministischer Rust-Regressionsfall speist die **vier echten beobachteten Fenstergrößen, Patchzahlen und Zeitspannen** des ersten Durchlaufs (12/18/8/17 Songs und 12,537/12,052/17,055/12,254 Sekunden) ohne Audio in denselben Controller. Er prüft die mögliche erste vollständige Grobsuche, die konkrete 48-Song-Budgetentscheidung und die erhaltene strikte Begrenzung wirklich kurzer Scan-Horizonte auf einem **anderen** Workerbereich. Für die künftige Diagnose enthalten die einzelnen Probe-Berichte `remaining_undispatched_songs` und das Scanner-JSON die aggregierte `last_exploration_budget` (Phase, offene Fenster, aktuell geschätztes Fenster, reservierte Exploit-Songs, angeforderte/verfügbare Songs). Diese neuen Tests sind **eine Regressionsprüfung der Steuerungslogik, kein erneuter Realmusik-Speedup**. Den vollständigen CI-Status nur vom *neuen* exakten Commit ableiten.

**Separater Diagnosepunkt:** Die bestehenden Autotune-Runs beobachteten bis zu **12 native Child-Prozesse** bei höchstens **6 gleichzeitig aktiven Analysen**. Das Produktions-Threadpool-Design erzeugt `max_workers` Threadslots, die Engines werden lazy und individuell beim ersten Job je Thread aufgebaut. Eine parallele Startkapazität 6 garantiert also **nicht**, dass im Verlauf nur sechs Native-Child-Sessions entstanden sind. Das kann zusätzlichen RAM kosten; genaue zeitliche Attribution und eine sichere Begrenzung von *initialisierten Sessions* erfordern eine eigene Untersuchung nach dem Suchbudget-Fix.
